About

I am a researcher in mathematical machine learning. I have completed my doctoral research and submitted my Ph.D. thesis in Computer Science at the Free University of Bozen–Bolzano, Italy; the remaining final examination and degree conferral are formal completion steps. My research develops function-space foundations for hierarchical and compositional learning, with particular emphasis on functional analysis, kernel methods, approximation theory, statistical learning theory, and optimization.

Before beginning my doctoral research in Computer Science, I completed a Ph.D. in Mathematics, specializing in Functional Analysis, at Islamic Azad University of Mashhad in 2018.

My recent work develops Brownian Kernel Ladders (BKL) , a recursive function-space framework based on Brownian kernels and hierarchical representations. This work has evolved into a broader research programme concerning variation spaces, recursive reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, finite-dimensional approximation, statistical complexity, and optimization of hierarchical learning models.

My current research focuses on the following areas:

  • Mathematical foundations of hierarchical and compositional learning
    • function-space perspectives on depth and hierarchical representation
    • approximation properties of recursive learning models
    • statistical complexity and generalization in high-dimensional settings
    • interactions among architecture, function space, and representation complexity
  • Brownian kernels, variation spaces, and optimization
    • Brownian Kernel Ladders and recursive Brownian RKHS constructions
    • Variation Brownian Kernel Ladders
    • measure-valued and finite-atomic representations
    • finite-dimensional Brownian RKHS approximation
    • optimization geometry and nonsmooth optimization
  • Theory-guided representation learning for Earth Observation
    • hyperspectral representation learning
    • high-dimensional spectral and spatial structure
    • multimodal and multiscale Earth Observation data
    • mathematically principled learning under limited annotations
    • connections between learning theory and practical remote-sensing models

Much of my research is motivated by a basic question: how can the empirical power of hierarchical learning systems be understood at the level of function spaces? Rather than viewing depth only as a property of a parameterized architecture, I study how recursive constructions influence approximation, statistical complexity, representation, and optimization.

My long-term goal is to develop a unified mathematical theory of hierarchical representation learning that is rigorous enough to explain model behavior and flexible enough to guide practical learning systems. I am particularly interested in developing these ideas in Earth Observation, where high-dimensional data, multiscale structure, heterogeneous sensing modalities, and limited annotations provide a natural setting for mathematically principled machine learning.

I am currently open to postdoctoral and research opportunities in mathematical machine learning, learning theory, kernel methods, representation learning, optimization, and theory-guided Earth Observation.
