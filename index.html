About

I am a researcher in mathematical machine learning. My research develops function-space foundations for hierarchical and compositional learning, using tools from functional analysis, kernel methods, approximation theory, statistical learning theory, and optimization.

I completed my doctoral work in Computer Science at the Free University of Bozen–Bolzano, Italy. Before this, I completed a Ph.D. in Mathematics, specializing in Functional Analysis, at Islamic Azad University of Mashhad in 2018.

A central part of my research is Brownian Kernel Ladders (BKL), a recursive hierarchy of integral reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces generated by Brownian pullback kernels. The framework separates two complementary levels: a full adaptive canonical envelope for representational geometry and variational analysis, and realized or finite controlled families for statistically accountable estimation.

My research focuses on the following areas:

  • Mathematical foundations of hierarchical and compositional learning
    • function-space perspectives on depth and hierarchical representation
    • approximation properties of recursive learning models
    • statistical complexity and generalization in high-dimensional settings
    • interactions among architecture, function space, and representation complexity
  • Brownian kernels, adaptive function spaces, and optimization
    • Brownian Kernel Ladders and recursive Brownian integral RKHSs
    • adaptive canonical envelopes and infimal function-space complexity
    • regularity, nestedness, and strict growth with depth
    • realized ladders and controlled representation dictionaries
    • Gaussian complexity, oracle inequalities, and excess-risk bounds
    • finite-dimensional approximation and nonsmooth optimization
  • Theory-guided representation learning for Earth Observation
    • hyperspectral representation learning
    • high-dimensional spectral and spatial structure
    • multimodal and multiscale Earth Observation data
    • mathematically principled learning under limited annotations
    • connections between learning theory and practical remote-sensing models

Much of my research is motivated by a basic question: how can the empirical power of hierarchical learning systems be understood at the level of function spaces? Rather than viewing depth only as a property of a parameterized architecture, I study how recursive constructions influence approximation, regularity, statistical complexity, representation, and optimization.

My broader goal is to develop a unified mathematical theory of hierarchical representation learning that is rigorous enough to explain model behavior and flexible enough to guide practical learning systems. High-dimensional, multiscale, and heterogeneous Earth Observation data provide a natural setting in which to develop and test these ideas.
