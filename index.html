01Research

Learning through function-space geometry.

My research connects functional analysis with statistical learning theory. I use reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs), variation spaces, and operator methods to study representation, approximation, generalization, and the dynamics of optimization.

  1. Recursive RKHSs & variation spaces

    Brownian Kernel Ladders encode depth through recursive Brownian geometry. I study their regularity, approximation properties, variational structure, and learning guarantees. Variation Brownian Kernel Ladders separate nonlinear recursion from outermost signed-measure superposition, supporting compactness, attainment, and constructive finite approximation.

    Brownian Kernel Ladders ↗Variation BKL ↗

  2. Adaptive representations & covariance geometry

    What is the statistical cost of choosing a representation? I study activation-mass characterizations for Brownian heads, same-sample selection through threshold traces, and common-covariance envelopes connecting Gaussian complexity, simultaneous approximation, and semidefinite certification.

    Common covariance geometry ↗Brownian heads ↗

  3. Representation & optimization

    I investigate how different finite realizations of the same function space affect optimizer trajectories. Current work examines Brownian-metric preconditioning, basis dependence, observable quotient geometry, and relaxed multilayer dynamics, combining mathematical analysis with controlled computational studies.

    Optimization geometry ↗

  4. Operator methods for multitask learning

    I study shared representations and task relationships through functions and operators. This includes Koopman- and composition-operator generalization bounds for deep multi-output models, vector-valued RKHSs, and trace-norm geometry.

    Operator-theoretic generalization ↗Multitask trace-norm preprint ↗
